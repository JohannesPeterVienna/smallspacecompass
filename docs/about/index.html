About & method

About Small Space Compass

Small Space Compass is a small-space planning site built around one idea: in a small home, the space you need to use furniture matters as much as the furniture itself. Every guide states the measurements it relies on, and every tool checks movement – door swings, pull-out zones, walkways – not just whether the pieces fit on the floor.

Who runs the site

The site is published by AdStern, an advertising agency based in Vienna, Austria (see the imprint). It is financed by advertising, which can appear on guide, tool, layout and overview pages, including the home page, the indexes and this page; the imprint, privacy policy and contact page show no ads. No manufacturer or retailer pays for mentions, and there are no affiliate links.

How the measurements are chosen

Where a published guideline exists, we use it and link the primary document:

  • Walkways and doors: the 2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design published by the U.S. Access Board – §403.5.1 (36 in clear width of walking surfaces), §404.2.3 (32 in door clear width and how to measure it), §304.3 (60 in turning space), §305.3 (30 × 48 in clear floor space) and §308 (15–48 in reach range).
  • Kitchens and bathrooms: the NKBA Kitchen Planning Guidelines and Bathroom Planning Guidelines of the National Kitchen & Bath Association, which also cite the International Residential Code where a code minimum applies.
  • Mattress sizes: U.S. sizes as listed in Casper's size chart, UK sizes as listed in Silentnight's size guide. Manufacturers vary by up to an inch, so always check the piece you are buying.

Where no published figure exists – the strip beside a bed, the depth of a drawer zone – we say that a number is a working assumption and explain how we arrived at it. Numbers we could not trace to a source are left out rather than repeated from other websites.

How the tools calculate

The Clearance Planner rasterizes the floor into cells of about one inch, marks furniture, use zones and door swings as blocked, and computes an exact Euclidean distance transform – the distance from every free point to the nearest obstacle. A bottleneck search finds the route from the entry to each target whose narrowest point is as wide as possible; an A* search then picks the shortest route of that width. The narrowest point is re-measured exactly between the two surfaces that cause it. Door swings are quarter circles tested exactly against each rectangle.

The delivery check tests every orientation of a box through doorways using the exact condition for one rectangle fitting inside another, and evaluates the classic “moving a sofa around a corner” formula numerically, including tilting the item within the ceiling height. The geometry code is covered by automated tests that compare results with hand-calculated cases and closed-form solutions.

Limits: a geometric aid only

The tools work with rectangles and quarter circles on a flat floor. They do not know about building codes, fire safety, structural loads, ventilation, wall anchors, the swing of window sashes, skirting boards, radiators or the weight of anything. A result that says “36 in” does not mean a home is accessible or compliant with any law; a delivery check that passes does not guarantee a piece can be carried in by two people. Check the manufacturer's dimensions, your lease and local rules before buying, drilling or building.

Your plans stay with you

Plans you draw are stored only in your own browser (localStorage) and are never sent to us. You can export them as SVG or JSON. Details on advertising and analytics are in the privacy policy.
