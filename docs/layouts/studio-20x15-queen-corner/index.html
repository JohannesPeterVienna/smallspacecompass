20 × 15 ft studio, queen bed in the far corner

The classic “bed away from the door” studio: the queen bed goes into the corner diagonally opposite the entry, the living area takes the window wall, and the desk sits at the foot of the bed where its chair does not cross the main route.

Why it is laid out this way

A 240 × 180 in (6.10 × 4.57 m) room is 300 sq ft – a common size for a U.S. studio. The entry door is 12 in from the left corner of the bottom wall and swings into the room, so the first 32 in quarter-circle inside the door has to stay empty. Everything else is arranged around one rule: the route from the door to the bed should not pass between the sofa and its coffee table.

The bed sits against the right-hand wall with its head at the top. Only one long side is free, so the bed carries a 24 in bedside zone on the left and a 24 in strip at its foot; both are walk-through zones that the route may use but furniture may not. The nightstand stands below the bed against the wall rather than beside the pillow, which keeps the bedside zone clear – a compromise you might reverse if you prefer a lamp at arm's length.

The sofa runs along the left wall, facing a TV unit that also works as a low room divider between living and sleeping areas. The coffee table keeps an 18 in legroom gap from the sofa. The desk is in the bottom-right corner; its 32 in chair zone points up into the room, away from the door swing.

The measured results above show the width of the widest route to each target, and the markers on the plan show where each route is tightest. Those marked points are the first places to check if you choose a deeper sofa, a larger coffee table or a wider TV unit.
