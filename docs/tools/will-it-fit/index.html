Will It Fit? Delivery Check

Type in the outside dimensions of a sofa, mattress or wardrobe and the narrowest parts of the route into your home. The check tries every orientation – straight, on its side, on end, tilted – and draws the one that works.

What the check calculates

Getting a piece of furniture into a home is a sequence of bottlenecks: the building door, the apartment door, a hallway that turns 90°, a stair with a landing, the bedroom door. Each one is a different geometric problem, and the check treats them separately.

Doors

Through a doorway, only the cross-section of the item matters – its length is carried straight through. The check tries all three ways to turn the box (each dimension leading in turn) and tests whether the remaining two dimensions fit the clear width and height of the opening. Turning the cross-section diagonally helps when one side is long and the other thin, like a mattress or a door panel; the check uses the exact geometric condition for fitting one rectangle inside another at an angle rather than a rough rule of thumb. For sofas, it also applies the profile rule used by furniture makers: tipped onto its back or end, a sofa with a sloped back clears a doorway when its diagonal depth is smaller than the door's clear width. Measure that from the top back of the frame to the front of the seat.

A 90° corner

In a hallway that turns a corner, length suddenly matters. The longest rectangle of width w that can be swung flat around a right-angled corner between corridors of width a and b is the minimum over all angles θ of a/cos θ + b/sin θ − w(tan θ + cot θ). For a thin pole in a corner between two 36 in halls that is about 102 in; for a 34 in deep sofa it is much less. The check evaluates this formula numerically and also tries tilting the item upwards: lifting one end shortens its footprint, as long as the tilted piece still fits under the ceiling. The sketch shows the item at its tightest angle in the corner.

Stair landings

A U-shaped or L-shaped stair forces the same turn on the landing, usually with less headroom. Enter the stair width and the clear height at the turn; the check handles it like a corner with equal widths and that headroom.

Measuring the route

  1. Door clear width: open the door to 90° and measure from the face of the door to the opposite stop – the method in ADA 2010 §404.2.3. This is typically 2 in or more less than the door leaf. Taking the door off its hinges usually gains the thickness of the leaf.
  2. Door clear height: floor or threshold to the underside of the head stop.
  3. Hallway widths: wall to wall at the narrowest point before and after the corner – radiators, trim and light switches count.
  4. Ceiling or headroom: at the corner itself, including any light fitting or soffit.
  5. Item: the outside dimensions with packaging if it arrives boxed; feet and cushions that can be removed can be left out.

Reading the result

Each checkpoint shows whether it passes and how: which side leads, which side is vertical, how far the item has to be tilted and how much room is left. If a corner fails, the result says by how much the item is too long even when tilted within the ceiling height. That number tells you what to ask the retailer: a sofa with removable arms or legs, a split box spring, a modular or “sofa-in-a-box” design.

The form opens with a sofa that makes it: 84 in long, 35 in deep, 34 in high, 31 in diagonal depth, through a 32 in door and around a corner between two 36 in halls. Change the depth to 37 in – the worked example in the sofa guide – and the same sofa still passes the door but fails at the hallway corner by about an inch. Those 2 in of depth are exactly what to ask the retailer about.

Frequently asked questions

What is the diagonal depth of a sofa?

The distance from the top of the back frame to the front edge of the seat, measured in a straight line from the side. Many retailers list it; otherwise measure with a straightedge across the side profile.

Does the check account for people carrying the item?

No. It checks the rigid geometry of a box with the dimensions you enter. Allow a couple of inches for hands, straps and packaging.

Why does a mattress fit when its width is wider than my door?

A mattress is thin. Turned on its side and slightly diagonally it can pass an opening narrower than its width. Foam mattresses can also bend, which the check does not assume.

Can it check spiral stairs?

No. Spiral and winder stairs need a 3D model of the steps; use the landing setting only for straight flights with a flat landing.
