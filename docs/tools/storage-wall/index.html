Storage Wall Audit

How much of your storage can you actually reach? Draw the wall, add the units, and the audit splits their volume into three bands: below 15 in, 15–48 in, and above 48 in – the reach range from the ADA Standards.

Why three bands

Section 308 of the 2010 ADA Standards sets the unobstructed reach range for forward and side reach at 15 in minimum and 48 in maximum above the floor (§308.2.1 and §308.3.1). That is an accessibility minimum for unobstructed reach, written for wheelchair users; over an obstruction the high limit drops to 44 in (forward, reach depth over 20 in, §308.2.2) or 46 in (side, over 10 in, §308.3.2). The audit uses the band as a planning line, not as a reach guarantee. Above 48 in most adults can still reach, but less comfortably and not over a deep obstruction; below 15 in you bend or kneel.

The audit computes the gross volume of each unit (width × depth × height) and splits it at 15 and 48 in. It sorts volume by height only: obstacles in front of a unit, shelf depth and whether the back of a deep shelf is reachable are not checked. It does not subtract shelf thickness or drawer boxes either, so treat the figures as a comparison between arrangements rather than as a capacity a manufacturer would quote. The value of the tool is in seeing the share: a wall where 60 % of the volume sits above 48 in is a wall for suitcases and seasonal things, not for everyday use.

Equal compartments

The divider calculator below the unit settings was previously a separate site, Shelf Measure. It divides an inside opening into equal clear compartments: clear width = (opening − dividers × thickness) ÷ compartments. It also lists where to mark the left edge of each divider, measured from the left inside face. Typical board thicknesses are 3/4 in (19 mm) for plywood and 5/8 in (16 mm) for melamine shelving.

How to use the audit

  1. Measure the wall: width between corners or obstructions, and the ceiling height.
  2. For each unit, enter its distance from the left, width, bottom and top height above the floor, and inside depth. Wall shelves start at their mounting height.
  3. List the shelf heights above the floor so the drawing shows them; the volume does not depend on them.
  4. Move units and shelves until the everyday band holds what you use every day – see the guide to vertical storage by reach.

Frequently asked questions

What do 15 and 48 inches mean?

They are the low and high limits of the unobstructed reach range in ADA 2010 §308.2.1 (forward reach) and §308.3.1 (side reach): 15 in minimum and 48 in maximum above the floor.

Are the volumes usable capacity?

No, they are gross volumes of the unit outlines. They are best used to compare layouts.

Can I enter centimeters?

Yes, switch to cm. Volumes are then shown in liters.
