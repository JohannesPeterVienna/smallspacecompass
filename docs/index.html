Most room planners stop at “the bed fits on the floor”. Small Space Compass draws door swings, chair and drawer zones and the route from your door to the bed, desk and window – then measures its narrowest point and compares it with the 36 in clear width from the 2010 ADA Standards.

What the tools check

  1. Clearances. Furniture as measured rectangles, door swing arcs and pull-out zones for chairs, drawers and wardrobe doors. Overlaps turn signal red.
  2. Walkways. A distance field over the floor finds the widest route from the entry to every target and reports the pinch point in inches or centimeters.
  3. Deliveries. Straight, tilted or turned: the delivery check tests a sofa, mattress or wardrobe against the door, a 90° hallway corner and a stair landing.
  4. Fold-away days. Wall beds, sofa beds and drop-leaf tables are checked twice – in their day and their night position.

Why measurements, not mood boards

Small homes fail at the margins: the wardrobe door that hits the bed frame, the desk chair that has nowhere to roll back, the 30 in gap that felt fine in a showroom and is a sideways shuffle at home. Every guide on this site therefore states its numbers, says where they come from and shows them on a plan you can open and change. Where a number is a published guideline – the ADA walking-surface width, the NKBA kitchen aisles – we link the primary document. Where it is our own working assumption, such as a 24 in strip beside a bed, we say so.
