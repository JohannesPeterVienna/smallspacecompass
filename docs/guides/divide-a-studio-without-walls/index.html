How to divide a studio without walls

A studio feels bigger when sleeping, living and working each have their own zone. You do not need walls for that – but every divider takes floor, and some take the walkway with it.

Last reviewed: October 20265 min readUnits: in + cm

What a divider has to do

A divider separates zones visually and, ideally, keeps the bed out of sight from the door. It should not block daylight, ventilation or the route from the entry to the bed and bathroom. Measured against those three tests, the six common options differ a lot.

1. A bookcase at right angles to the wall

The most effective divider: a 12 in deep bookcase standing out from the wall creates an alcove and holds books on both sides. Open shelving keeps light; a back panel on the lower half and open shelves above is a good compromise. Make sure the opening between the end of the bookcase and the opposite wall or furniture stays at least 36 in wide – the walkway width from ADA 2010 §403.5.1 – and anchor the bookcase to the ceiling or wall. A freestanding tall bookcase standing on its narrow side tips easily.

2. The sofa back

A sofa that floats with its back to the bed costs no extra floor: you need the sofa anyway. It divides at seat-back height, about 30–34 in, which separates zones without blocking anything. A narrow console table or desk against the back adds storage – see desk in a studio. The cost: the walkway now runs around the sofa on at least one side, and the sofa's legroom zone has to stay clear in front.

3. A curtain on a ceiling track

A curtain is the only divider that takes almost no floor and disappears in the day. Mount the track on the ceiling, at least a few inches from the bed so the curtain does not drag over it, and leave the end towards the walkway open or tied back. In a rental, tension rods or a track on removable fixings avoid drilling. A heavy, lined curtain also blocks light from a desk lamp or a TV.

4. Rugs

A rug divides by suggestion: the living zone is the area on the rug. It takes no floor and costs no walkway, which is why rugs are the first tool in very small studios. Size matters – a rug that is too small makes a zone look smaller. The rug size guide has measured examples.

5. A raised platform

A platform 12–18 in high for the bed or the living zone separates zones strongly and can hold drawers underneath. It takes the whole floor of its zone and adds a step to every route, which matters at night. It also changes the height of everything: a window sill, an outlet, the ceiling above the bed. Building one in a rental usually needs permission.

6. Low open shelving

A row of low cubes (about 30–36 in high) divides like a sofa back and holds books, plants and baskets on both sides. It is lighter than a full-height bookcase and safer, but still needs the 36 in opening at its end.

Three rules for any divider

  1. Never across the only route. The walkway from the door to the bed, kitchen and bathroom must go around the divider with at least 36 in.
  2. Not in front of the window unless it is open shelving or a curtain that is pulled back in the day.
  3. Mind the door swing. A divider within the entry door's arc will be hit every day; keep it at least the leaf width away from the hinge.

Measuring for a divider

Before you buy, draw the room and mark: the door swing, the bed with its 24 in bedside strip, the walkway from the entry, and the window. The divider can go anywhere outside those zones. For a bookcase, the length you can use is the distance from the wall to the start of the walkway; for a curtain, the length of the bed plus its strip.

Frequently asked questions

How do I divide a studio apartment cheaply?

A rug and a curtain on a ceiling track cost little, take almost no floor and need no tools beyond a drill or tension rods. A sofa placed with its back to the bed is free.

Can I use a bookcase as a room divider?

Yes, ideally an open-back bookcase about 12 in deep, anchored to the wall or ceiling. Keep at least 36 in between its end and the next obstacle.

How do I hide a bed in a studio?

Put the bed in the corner furthest from the door and screen it with a bookcase at right angles to the wall, or a ceiling-mounted curtain.

Do room dividers make a studio feel smaller?

Tall, solid dividers can. Low or open dividers and curtains keep sight lines and light and usually make the room feel larger by giving each zone its own edge.

Check your own room: try a divider in the Clearance Planner – a bookshelf preset rotated 90° works as a half wall.
