Desk in a studio: 5 spots that don’t block the door

A desk needs about 32 in behind it for the chair. Where that chair zone lands decides whether the desk works in a studio. Five spots that keep it out of the door swing and the main route.

Last reviewed: October 20265 min readUnits: in + cm

The chair is the problem, not the desk

32 inChair zone behind a desk (813 mm)by analogy with NKBA guideline 8: table edge to wall, no traffic

A 42 × 22 in desk takes 6.4 sq ft. The chair that goes with it needs room to pull out and sit down: this site plans 32 in from the desk edge, the figure the NKBA Kitchen Planning Guidelines give for the space between a table edge and a wall where nobody passes (guideline 8). If people walk behind the chair, that grows to 36 in to edge past and 44 in to walk past. The chair zone is therefore larger than the desk – and in a studio it is the chair zone that collides with door swings and walkways.

1. The corner furthest from the door

The default answer. In a corner the desk has walls on two sides, so the chair zone points into the room on one side only. Pick the corner diagonally opposite the door and the zone almost never meets the route. A corner desk (L-shaped) also works here, but it doubles the chair zone if you use both sides.

2. At the foot of the bed

If the bed is in a corner with its foot towards the room, a desk at the foot can share the bed's foot strip: the chair backs into the space you already keep free to walk round the bed. This is the arrangement in the corner queen studio. It works when the foot strip is at least as deep as the chair zone; if it is only 24 in, the chair will touch the bed.

3. Behind the sofa

A sofa that floats in the middle of the room has a back that is otherwise wasted. A narrow desk (18–22 in deep) against it doubles as a divider between living and sleeping zones. The chair then sits on the far side, so check that the zone does not end in the walkway. This spot needs a room at least about 13 ft deep in that direction: sofa 36 in + desk 22 in + chair 32 in + legroom 18 in + TV unit 16 in + walkway.

4. The window wall

Daylight from the side or front is ideal for a desk, and a window wall often has nothing else against it. Watch three things: a radiator under the window (keep the gap its manufacturer specifies), inward-opening sashes (the desk must not block them), and glare on the screen when the window is directly behind it. A desk at right angles to the window wall often works better than one facing the glass.

5. A closet or alcove

A reach-in closet about 24 in deep and 48 in or more wide can hold a desk with the doors removed or replaced by a curtain. The chair zone then lies entirely outside in the room, so treat it like a desk against a wall. You lose the closet as storage – compensate with the ideas in entryway storage without a closet.

Spots to avoid

  • Beside the entry door, facing the hinge side. The chair zone lands in the door swing; one of the two will hit the other every day.
  • In a straight walkway narrower than 36 + 32 = 68 in. The chair will be in the route whenever someone sits.
  • Facing the bed in a room under about 10 ft between them – work and sleep zones blur, and the chair hits the bed frame.

The chair zone more than doubles the footprint of any desk. A wall-mounted fold-down desk is the only kind that gives the whole area back when not in use – plan it like other fold-away furniture, in both states.

Light and power

A desk also needs an outlet and good light. Check where the outlets are before choosing a spot: an extension cord across a walkway is a trip hazard on the very route you are trying to keep clear. Natural light from the side – window to the left for right-handed people – avoids shadows on paper and glare on screens.

Frequently asked questions

How much space do you need behind a desk chair?

About 32 in from the desk edge to a wall or obstacle if nobody walks behind you; 36–44 in if people pass. This follows the NKBA table-seating guideline.

Where should a desk go in a studio apartment?

In the corner diagonally opposite the entry, at the foot of a corner bed, behind a floating sofa, on the window wall, or in a closet. Avoid placing it where the chair zone lands in the door swing.

How deep should a desk be for a small space?

18–22 in is enough for a laptop and a monitor arm. Each inch saved in depth adds to the walkway or the chair zone.

Can a desk go in front of a window?

Yes, if it does not block an inward-opening sash or crowd a radiator. Watch for screen glare.
