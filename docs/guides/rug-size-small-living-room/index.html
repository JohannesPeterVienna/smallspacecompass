Rug size guide for small living rooms

A rug that is too small makes a small room look smaller. The usual rule – front legs of the seating on the rug – translates into a few measurable distances. Here they are, with plans for three common sizes.

Rugs are sold in nominal sizes; actual sizes are often an inch or two smaller, and metric rugs (160 × 230 cm, 200 × 300 cm) are common in the UK and Europe. Measure the rug you are buying.

The rule, measured

For a seating area, the most useful rule is: the front legs of every seat stand on the rug. With a typical sofa 36 in deep and front legs about 4 in in from the front, the rug must reach at least 32 in from the wall behind the sofa. On the other sides, leave a band of floor between rug and walls – the rug then looks placed rather than trapped. These distances are design conventions, not standards; use them as a starting point.

5 × 8 ft in a 10 × 12 ft living area

A 5 × 8 ft rug is the most common size for small living rooms. Placed lengthways in front of a 78 in sofa, it extends about 9 in beyond each end of the sofa and carries the coffee table with about 28 in to spare on each side. The armchair's front legs are on it too. The band of floor to the walls makes the room look larger than a wall-to-wall rug would.

6 × 9 ft

A 6 × 9 ft rug suits a living area of about 11 × 13 ft and larger, or a sofa wider than about 84 in. It extends further under the seating and can hold a pair of chairs facing the sofa. In a smaller room it starts touching the walls and makes the floor look busy.

Rugs as zone markers in a studio

In a studio, a rug says “this is the living room”. Two rules make it work:

  • One rug per zone, clearly separate – a rug that runs under the bed and the sofa merges the two zones instead of dividing them.
  • Keep the walkway off the rug edge where possible, or choose a flat-weave rug with a thin edge so nobody trips at night.

See How to divide a studio without walls for rugs combined with other dividers.

Under a bed

Under a bed, a rug should extend beyond the sides where you get out: a 5 × 8 ft rug under a queen (60 × 80 in), placed crosswise with the bed's lower two-thirds on it, extends 18 in on each side – roughly the bedside strip. In a small bedroom, two runners beside the bed often work better than one large rug.

Safety and practicalities

  • Use a non-slip underlay; it also stops the rug creeping under a door.
  • Check the door: a thick rug inside a door swing can stop the door opening fully. Measure the gap under the door.
  • Rugs with a raised edge are a trip hazard on night routes.

Under a dining table

A rug under a dining table must be large enough that the chairs stay on it when pulled out – otherwise the back legs catch on the edge every time someone sits down. Add the chair zone on every side: for a 48 × 30 in table with 32 in zones on both long sides, the rug must be at least 30 + 2 × 32 = 94 in across. A 5 × 8 ft (60 in) or 6 × 9 ft (72 in) rug is too narrow in that direction; an 8 × 10 ft rug (96 in) is the smallest standard size that works. In small spaces, a bare floor under the table is often the better choice.

How to test a size before buying

Mark the rug outline on the floor with painter's tape and leave it for a day. Walk the routes, open the doors and sit on the sofa. If the tape crosses a doorway or ends just short of the sofa's front legs, change the size. In the Clearance Planner, the rug preset sits under the furniture and does not count as an obstacle, so you can try several sizes against the real plan.

Pile height in small rooms

In a small room, rugs sit on routes far more often than in a large one. A low pile or flat-weave rug lets doors swing over it, does not catch chair legs and is easier to keep clean. If a door has to open over the rug, measure the gap under the door and compare it with the rug's thickness plus underlay.

Frequently asked questions

What size rug for a small living room?

Usually 5 × 8 ft (60 × 96 in). It allows the front legs of a standard sofa and an armchair to stand on the rug while leaving a band of floor to the walls.

Should the sofa be on the rug?

At least its front legs. A rug that stops short of the sofa makes the seating area look smaller.

Is a 5x8 rug too small?

Not for a living area of about 10 × 12 ft with a sofa up to about 80 in long. For larger sofas or rooms, consider 6 × 9 ft.

How much floor should show around a rug?

In small rooms, roughly 8–18 in between rug and walls looks deliberate. It is a design convention, not a rule.
