Bedrooms

12 × 10 bedroom layout ideas (queen or full)

A 12 × 10 ft room is 120 sq ft – big enough for a queen bed with walking space on both sides, or a full bed and a desk. The deciding question is which wall the headboard goes against.

Last reviewed: October 20265 min readUnits: in + cm

The numbers that decide the layout

The room is 144 × 120 in (3.66 × 3.05 m). A U.S. queen mattress is 60 × 80 in and a full is 53 × 75 in, according to Casper’s size chart; bed frames typically add a little to each dimension, so measure yours. Subtracting the bed from the room gives what is left for walking and other furniture:

Bed and orientationLeft across the roomLeft at the foot
Queen, head on 10 ft wall144 − 80 = 64 in at the foot120 − 60 = 60 in beside
Queen, head on 12 ft wall144 − 60 = 84 in beside120 − 80 = 40 in at the foot
Full, head on 10 ft wall144 − 75 = 69 in at the foot120 − 53 = 67 in beside
Full, head on 12 ft wall144 − 53 = 91 in beside120 − 75 = 45 in at the foot

The rule this site uses for a bed you share or make every day is a 24 in strip on each side you use and at least a 36 in walkway where people pass (the clear width in ADA 2010 §403.5.1). Apply that to the table and two layouts stand out.

Layout A: queen bed, headboard on the 10 ft wall

With the headboard on a short wall, the queen's 80 in length runs along the 12 ft dimension. The 60 in left across the room is split into two 24 in bedside strips plus nightstands at the head, with 12 in to spare; 64 in remain at the foot. That is enough for the door, a walkway and a dresser with its drawers open.

Put the door on the wall at the foot end if you can choose, and make sure its swing ends against a wall rather than the dresser. In the plan above, the door is on the long wall near the corner and swings into the corner, so nothing is inside its arc.

Layout B: queen bed, headboard on the 12 ft wall

Centring the bed on the long wall looks symmetrical and leaves 42 in on each side, but only 40 in at the foot. That is a walkway, not a furniture zone. A dresser has to go on a side wall, where its 18 in drawer zone meets the bedside strip. It works if the dresser is shallow and placed beside the head of the bed, but the room will feel narrower.

Layout C: full bed in the corner plus a desk

A full bed pushed into the corner gives up access from one side but frees about 8 sq ft of floor compared with a centered queen – enough for a 42 in desk with a 32 in chair zone. This layout suits a single sleeper or a guest room that doubles as a home office. The route to the window is the tight one here; with the desk chair pushed in it is acceptable.

The door decides more than the bed

A standard 30 in interior door swinging into the room claims a quarter circle with a 30 in radius – about 4.9 sq ft that must stay empty. In a 120 sq ft room that is 4 % of the floor. Three ways to manage it:

  • Hinge the door on the side nearest a corner so that it opens against the wall.
  • Keep the arc as part of the walkway – the area you walk through anyway.
  • If the arc lands on the only good wall for the bed, consider a sliding door; check your lease first.

Windows and radiators

A headboard under a window is possible if the sill is higher than the headboard and there is no radiator. Otherwise, use the window wall for a desk or dresser. Keep at least one route to the window wide enough to open it and reach the latch – the planner measures the route to every window you draw.

Before you buy the bed

  1. Measure the room inside wall faces and note the door hinge side and swing.
  2. Measure the bed frame, not just the mattress – frames with footboards and storage drawers can be several inches larger.
  3. If the frame has drawers, add an 18 in drawer zone on that side.
  4. Check the delivery route for the frame and mattress with the five delivery measurements.

Frequently asked questions

Can a queen bed fit in a 12x10 room?

Yes. With the headboard on a 10 ft wall a queen (60 × 80 in) leaves 64 in at the foot and room for 24 in strips on both sides plus nightstands.

Which wall should the bed go on in a 12x10 room?

Usually a 10 ft wall. It leaves more space at the foot for a dresser and the door; with the headboard on the 12 ft wall only 40 in remain at the foot of a queen.

Can I fit a desk in a 12x10 bedroom with a queen bed?

Only a small one, typically beside the dresser at the foot end, and only if the door swing is not in the way. With a full bed in the corner, a 42 in desk fits comfortably.

Is a 12x10 bedroom small?

At 120 sq ft it is a common size for a secondary bedroom. It fits a queen bed with access from both sides, which is the main test of a usable double bedroom.
