Studio apartment layouts: 6 measured floor plans

Six common studio shapes, each drawn to the inch with door swings, chair and drawer zones and the narrowest point between the entry and the bed, desk and window. Open any of them in the planner and change the walls to match yours.

Last reviewed: October 20265 min readUnits: in + cm

How these plans were made

Every plan uses real furniture sizes: U.S. mattress sizes from Casper’s size chart (full 53 × 75 in, queen 60 × 80 in), and common retail sizes for the rest, which you can change in the planner. Each piece carries its use zone – 32 in for a chair at a desk or table, 18 in for drawers, 24 in beside a bed – and every door is drawn with its swing. The yellow line is the widest route from the entry to each target; the marker is where it is narrowest, measured. The table on each layout page compares those widths with the 36 in walking-surface width from ADA 2010 §403.5.1.

1. Queen bed in the far corner (20 × 15 ft)

The most robust studio layout: put the bed in the corner furthest from the entry, so that the route to it crosses the living area at its edge rather than through the middle. The bed needs only one long side free in a studio; push the other against the wall and keep a 24 in strip on the open side and at the foot. The living area takes the window wall. The desk sits at the foot of the bed, chair zone pointing into the room and away from the door swing.

Works for: roughly square rooms from about 250 sq ft. Watch: the gap between sofa legroom and TV unit – it is the first route to shrink if you buy a deeper sofa.

2. Full bed in an alcove (18 × 14 ft)

A tall bookcase standing at right angles to the wall creates a sleeping alcove without building anything. It works best with a full bed: a queen leaves too little space between the bed and the shelf for a comfortable bedside strip. The bookcase must be anchored to the wall or ceiling.

Works for: rooms where the bed can go in a corner away from the window. Watch: do not let the alcove block daylight; use an open-back shelf if the window is behind it. More options in How to divide a studio without walls.

3. Long, narrow studio in three zones (22 × 12 ft)

In a narrow room, the walkway should run straight along one long wall, and the big pieces along the other. Arrange the zones in the order you use them from the door: work first, living in the middle, sleeping at the far end. Avoid deep furniture on both long walls – two 36 in deep pieces opposite each other in a 144 in room leave 72 in, which sounds generous until you add legroom and a coffee table.

Works for: rooms more than about 1.6 times as long as they are wide. Watch: anything placed on the route side; keep it 16 in deep or less.

4. Queen wall bed (15 × 15 ft)

A wall bed gives back most of the floor in the day – this plan keeps 77 % free – but only if nothing permanent stands in front of it. Place the sofa beside the bed's open footprint, not in front of it; then only a light coffee table has to move each evening. The layout page shows the day and night plans side by side.

Works for: square rooms from about 200 sq ft, people who will actually fold the bed up every day. See Murphy bed room size.

5. Studio with a kitchen wall (16 × 20 ft)

When the kitchen is a single run of cabinets in the room, the space in front of it is a work aisle. The NKBA recommends 42 in for one cook (Kitchen Planning Guideline 6); treat that strip as a no-furniture zone you can walk through. The bed goes in the corner furthest from the cooking area, the dining table between them.

Works for: rectangular studios with a kitchen on a long wall. Watch: the fridge door swing near the entry.

6. Sofa bed, no separate bed (24 × 14 ft)

The cheapest way to free floor space: one piece of furniture for sitting and sleeping. The trade-off is time and bedding storage, not space – a sofa bed opens about as far as a wall bed. The window route is the tight one in this plan, which the layout page shows in its route table.

Works for: wide rooms where the sofa can open without hitting the dining or desk area. Compare in Wall bed vs. sofa bed.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best layout for a studio apartment?

For most roughly square studios: bed in the corner furthest from the entry, living area along the window wall, and the desk where its chair does not cross the route from the door. Long narrow studios work better as three zones in a row.

How big is a typical studio apartment?

The plans here range from 225 to 336 sq ft (about 21 to 31 m²). The layout principles apply to smaller rooms as well, but you may need fold-away furniture below about 220 sq ft.

Can I fit a queen bed in a studio?

Usually yes – a queen is 60 × 80 in. What matters is the space around it: with one long side against a wall you need a 24 in strip on the other side and at the foot, plus the walkway.

Can I use these plans for my own apartment?

Yes. Open the layout in the planner, enter your own wall lengths and door position, and move the pieces until every route is wide enough.

Check your own room: every plan opens in the Clearance Planner or the Day/Night Floor Swap from its layout page.
