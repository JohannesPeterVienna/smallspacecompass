Storage

Entryway storage for apartments without a closet

Many studios open straight into the room. Without a closet, the first few feet inside the door have to hold coats, shoes, bags and keys – without stealing the door swing or the walkway.

Last reviewed: October 20265 min readUnits: in + cm

Start with the door

The entry door decides where storage can go. A 32 in door swinging into the room claims a quarter circle with a 32 in radius on the hinge side – nothing deeper than the door's own clearance can stand there. On the latch side, the space beside the door frame is where you naturally stand when you come in; that is the best place for hooks and a shoe bench. Measure:

  • the wall length on the latch side, from the door frame to the next corner or obstacle;
  • the wall length on the hinge side behind the open door;
  • the walkway from the door into the room – it should stay at least 36 in wide, the clear width in ADA 2010 §403.5.1.

The reach range of 15–48 in from ADA 2010 §308.2.1 is the zone for things you grab every day – keys, bags, a dog lead. Coat hooks are an exception: long coats need to hang above the floor, so adult hooks go higher. Mount a second row lower for children, guests or short jackets. See Vertical storage by reach for the reasoning.

Depth is the constraint

Everything at an entry sits next to a walkway. A coat on a hook projects 10–12 in from the wall; a shoe bench is typically 12–16 in deep; a shoe cabinet with tilt-out drawers only 7–9 in when closed. In a narrow entry corridor, those inches decide whether the walkway stays at 36 in:

Seven ideas, from shallow to deep

  1. A rail of hooks along the latch-side wall: no floor at all.
  2. A slim shoe cabinet (tilt-out drawers) behind the door's swing arc – only if the door still opens fully past it.
  3. A shoe bench with a shelf underneath: somewhere to sit, about 4–6 pairs of shoes.
  4. A wall shelf with hooks above the bench: hats, gloves and a key tray at reach height.
  5. Over-door hooks on the inside of the entry door: a few bags or scarves, no wall needed.
  6. A freestanding coat stand in a corner: easy to move, but it takes about 20 in square.
  7. A shallow wardrobe (16–18 in) if the entry is wide enough: closed storage for coats and a vacuum.

How much shoe storage do you need?

Count the pairs in daily use, not all you own. Rough capacities: a 36 in wide shelf holds 4–5 pairs of adult shoes; a tilt-out drawer about 3 pairs. Keep only those at the door; the rest go in the high or low band elsewhere.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Hooks so close to the door frame that coats are hit by the opening door.
  • A bench or cabinet inside the door swing – the door will stop against it.
  • A mirror at the only spot where you stand to put on shoes.
  • Deep storage on both sides of a narrow entry: the walkway becomes a squeeze.

Using the hinge side

The wall behind an open door is often wasted. Measure the distance between the face of the fully open door and the wall it opens towards: that gap – often 2–4 in with a door stop – is the maximum depth of anything you mount there. Shallow hooks, a slim key cabinet or a fold-down shelf can use it. Anything deeper stops the door short of fully open, which reduces the clear width of the entrance – the width that every delivery has to pass, as explained in the sofa delivery guide.

Plan it before you drill

Draw the entry wall in the Storage Wall Audit: wall width, bench, cabinet and shelf heights. The tool shows how much of the storage sits within the 15–48 in band. Then draw the floor plan in the Clearance Planner with the door swing, and add the bench with a 24 in sitting zone in front – the space you need to sit and put on shoes. If the zone and the door swing overlap, the bench is in the wrong place.

Frequently asked questions

How high should coat hooks be?

About 60–66 in for adult coats so long coats do not drag; a second row at 40–48 in for children, guests or bags.

How deep should an entryway bench be?

Usually 12–16 in. In a narrow entry, check that the walkway beside it stays at least 36 in wide.

Where do I put shoes without a closet?

In a slim tilt-out cabinet or under a bench by the door, keeping only the pairs you use daily there.

Can storage go behind the entry door?

Only if it is shallow enough that the door still opens fully – measure the gap between the open door and the wall.

Check your own room: audit your entry wall in the Storage Wall Audit; the default example is a coat and shoe wall.
