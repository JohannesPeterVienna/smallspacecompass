Wall bed vs. sofa bed: floor space compared

Both give back floor space in the day, but in different ways. A wall bed disappears into an 18 in cabinet and leaves the room empty; a sofa bed keeps a sofa in the room and opens about as far. Here is the same room planned both ways.

The open depths are close: the difference between the two is mostly what happens in the day. The wall bed leaves 18 in; the sofa bed is a sofa, 36 in deep, plus the 18 in of legroom in front of it that the room needs anyway.

The same room, both ways

The plans below use a 15 × 15 ft (180 × 180 in) studio with the entry in the bottom-right corner. In both, a coffee table is the only “day only” piece; the rest stays in place at night.

At night the two rooms are almost identical. In the day, the wall-bed room has more free floor because there is no sofa – the loveseat in that plan is a separate, smaller piece of furniture. If you need a sofa anyway, a sofa bed is the more space-efficient choice: one piece instead of two.

What has to happen every evening

  • Wall bed: clear the floor in front of it (coffee table, rug with furniture on it), lower the bed. Bedding stays on the mattress; a good mechanism takes seconds. The cabinet front must be free of anything that blocks it opening – a lamp or plant on the floor in front of it is the usual culprit.
  • Sofa bed: remove seat cushions (and find a place for them), pull out the frame, add or unfold bedding. Bedding needs a storage place in the day – a cupboard or the sofa's own bedding box, if it has one.

If the bed will be used every night, the wall bed's speed matters more than anything else. Many sofa beds end up permanently open because folding them away is too much work.

Mattress and sleep

A vertical queen wall bed takes a standard queen mattress (60 × 80 in according to Casper’s size chart), often with a maximum thickness set by the cabinet. Sofa beds usually use a thinner mattress that folds with the frame, and many are narrower than a full. For nightly use, the wall bed wins on comfort; for a guest bed used a few nights a year, a good sofa bed is enough.

Installation and renting

A wall bed must be anchored to the wall or have a freestanding frame designed for that purpose. In a rental, ask the landlord before drilling, and check what the wall is made of. A sofa bed needs no installation, moves with you and is easier to sell – see Moving into a studio for which pieces are worth taking along.

Which one to choose

  • Sleeping in the studio every night, wall you can anchor to: wall bed.
  • Guest bed, or you need a sofa anyway: sofa bed.
  • Room under about 200 sq ft: wall bed with a small loveseat beside it, so the floor in front stays clear.
  • Very low ceilings or sloped walls: a horizontal wall bed (side-tilt) needs less height but more wall length.

Frequently asked questions

Does a wall bed save more space than a sofa bed?

In the day, yes – a wall bed cabinet is about 18 in deep, a sofa about 36 in. At night both open to around 86–90 in from the wall. If you need a sofa anyway, a sofa bed saves one piece of furniture.

How far does a queen Murphy bed stick out?

Typically around 86 in from the wall when open (cabinet plus bed). Check the specific model.

Is a wall bed comfortable for every night?

It takes a regular mattress, often up to a stated maximum thickness, so it can be as comfortable as a normal bed. Most sofa beds use thinner folding mattresses.

Can you put a wall bed in a rental?

Only with permission if it has to be anchored to the wall; some models have freestanding frames.
