How much space between bed and wall?

Leave about 24 in beside every side of the bed you use and 36 in wherever it is also a walkway. Pushing one side against the wall saves space but makes the bed harder to make. Here is how to decide, with measurements.

Last reviewed: October 20265 min readUnits: in + cm

Short answer

24 inBedside strip on each side you use (610 mm)site planning assumption – see reasoning below

There is no building code for the space beside a bed. The 24 in figure used on this site is a planning assumption: it is enough to stand facing the bed, bend to tuck in a sheet and get in and out without turning sideways. Where the strip is also the way to somewhere else – a window, a wardrobe, the bathroom – it becomes a walkway, and this site uses the 36 in clear width from ADA 2010 §403.5.1 as the reference for that.

Beside the bed

The space beside a bed is used in three ways: standing to make the bed, getting in and out, and reaching a nightstand. A nightstand typically stands within the strip at the head of the bed – the strip is about standing room, and a nightstand at the head does not take it away because you stand beside the mattress, not beside the nightstand.

At the foot of the bed

The foot of the bed is usually where people walk. If nothing else is there, 24 in is enough to walk round; if it is the main route from the door, plan 36 in. If a dresser stands opposite the foot, add its drawer zone: about 18 in for the drawer and to stand in front of it. A queen with a dresser at the foot therefore needs around 80 + 36 + 18 + 18 = 152 in of room length – more than a 12 ft (144 in) room has. That is why the dresser in a 12 × 10 ft room usually goes beside the door rather than opposite the bed.

Pushing the bed against a wall

Putting one long side of the bed against the wall saves one whole bedside strip – 24 in times the bed length, or about 13 sq ft for a queen. It is the standard trick in studios and children's rooms. The costs:

  • Making the bed is harder; fitted sheets on the wall side are the worst part. A light frame you can pull out a few inches helps.
  • Two sleepers share one exit. Many couples will not accept this for a main bedroom.
  • Cold exterior walls can cause condensation behind the mattress; leave a gap of an inch or two for air.

Pushing the head of the bed into a corner, so that only one long side and the foot are open, combines both savings. It works best for a single bed or a full used by one person.

Behind the headboard

A bed does not need space behind the headboard, but a few details change the numbers. A radiator under the window behind the bed needs air; leave the gap the radiator manufacturer recommends. Wall sockets and switches behind the headboard should stay reachable. A headboard that is thicker than the frame adds to the bed length – measure the whole bed, not the mattress.

How to check your own room

  1. Measure the room and the bed frame including headboard and footboard.
  2. Decide which sides you will use daily; give each a 24 in strip.
  3. Mark every route that crosses a strip – to the window, the wardrobe, the door – and give it 36 in.
  4. Add drawer and door-swing zones of anything facing the bed.
  5. Draw it in the planner; it will show the narrowest point of every route.

Frequently asked questions

How much space should be on each side of a bed?

About 24 in on each side you use to get in and make the bed; 36 in if that side is also a walkway to a window, wardrobe or door. This is a planning assumption, not a code requirement.

Is it OK to push a bed against the wall?

Yes, especially for a single sleeper or a studio. It saves about 13 sq ft with a queen bed but makes the bed harder to make, and on cold exterior walls you should leave a small gap for air.

How much space at the foot of the bed?

At least 24 in to walk round, 36 in if it is the main route, plus 18 in for each drawer zone if a dresser faces the bed.

What size room do I need for a queen bed with two nightstands?

Across the bed: 60 in mattress + 2 × 24 in strips = 108 in, so a 9 ft wall is the practical minimum. Along the bed: 80 in plus at least 24–36 in at the foot.
