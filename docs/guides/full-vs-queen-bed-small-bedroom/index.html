Full vs. queen bed in a small bedroom

On paper a queen is only 7 in wider and 5 in longer than a full. In a small bedroom those inches come out of the strips you walk in, and they decide whether a dresser or desk still fits.

Last reviewed: October 20265 min readUnits: in + cm

The sizes

FullQueenDifference
Width53 in (134.6 cm)60 in (152.4 cm)+7 in
Length75 in (190.5 cm)80 in (203.2 cm)+5 in
Area3,975 sq in (27.6 sq ft)4,800 sq in (33.3 sq ft)+825 sq in (+5.7 sq ft)
Width per sleeper (two)26.5 in30 in+3.5 in

Mattress dimensions are the U.S. standard sizes from Casper’s size chart. Frames add to these, sometimes several inches with a footboard or upholstered headboard. The mattress area difference is 825 sq in, about 5.7 sq ft. But the bed's footprint is not the whole story – the floor around it changes too.

What the difference does to the room

A bed with two usable sides and a walkable foot needs a 24 in strip on each side and at least 24 in at the foot (see space between bed and wall). The total space a bed claims, mattress plus strips, is therefore:

BedWidth with two 24 in stripsLength with 24 in at footTotal area
Full101 in99 in69.4 sq ft
Queen108 in104 in78.0 sq ft

The real difference is about 8.6 sq ft once access is included – one and a half times the mattress difference. In a 120 sq ft room that is 7 % of the floor.

Room sizes where the choice flips

Use the wall the headboard goes against and the wall along the bed:

  • Headboard wall under 9 ft (108 in): a queen cannot have two 24 in strips. Choose a full, or push the queen against a wall.
  • Headboard wall 9–10 ft: a queen fits with two strips, but nightstands wider than about 6 in each will intrude on the strips (108 in + 2 × 6 in = 120 in).
  • Bed-length wall under 9 ft: with a queen, only 28 in remain at the foot – a walkway, but no dresser opposite.
  • Both walls 10 ft or more: a queen with two strips, nightstands and a dresser works if the door swing is clear.

Who needs the extra width

A full gives two sleepers 26.5 in each – about the width of a twin mattress cut in half and a little more. Many couples find that tight; many single sleepers find a full generous. Practical guidance:

  • One sleeper: a full is usually enough and leaves floor for a desk or a reading chair.
  • Two sleepers who both get up at night: a queen with two strips beats a full pushed against a wall, even if the room is tight.
  • Guest room used a few nights a year: a full, or a wall bed or sofa bed.
  • Tall sleepers: the queen's extra 5 in of length matters more than its width; a full is 75 in long.

UK sizes

UK beds are measured in centimeters and named differently. According to Silentnight’s size guide, a UK double is 135 × 190 cm (about 53.1 × 74.8 in) – almost exactly a U.S. full. A UK king is 150 × 200 cm (about 59.1 × 78.7 in), close to a U.S. queen. The same reasoning applies: the step from double to king costs about 15 cm across and 10 cm along the bed, plus the access strips.

Delivery and moving

A queen mattress is 60 in wide and a full 53 in, but both are thin. On its side and slightly diagonal, a mattress passes through ordinary doorways; the problem is usually a box spring, which does not bend. Split box springs for queen beds exist for exactly this reason. Check stair landings with the delivery check.

Decision in four steps

  1. Measure the headboard wall and the wall along the bed.
  2. Subtract the bed plus 24 in per used side and at the foot.
  3. Check that the remaining floor still holds the door swing and any drawer zones.
  4. If the queen leaves a walkway under 32 in anywhere, choose the full – or push the queen to the wall and accept a one-side bed.

Frames change the comparison

The mattress sizes above are the starting point; the frame decides the footprint. Three frame features add the most:

  • Footboards and sleigh ends add 2–6 in to the length. On a queen in a 9 ft deep room, that is the difference between a 28 in and a 22 in strip at the foot.
  • Upholstered headboards add 2–4 in to the length and often 1–2 in to the width on each side.
  • Storage drawers under the bed add a drawer zone on each side with drawers. A drawer that pulls out 18 in into a 24 in bedside strip leaves 6 in – you cannot stand there while it is open. Choose a frame with drawers at the foot, or lift-up storage, if the side strips are tight.

A minimal platform frame adds almost nothing and is the best choice when a queen only just fits. When comparing, write down the outside frame dimensions of both candidates and redo the arithmetic above with those numbers rather than with the mattress sizes.

Bedding and everyday use

The size you choose also decides the bedding you buy and store. Queen bedding is the most widely available size in the U.S.; full bedding is cheaper but the range is smaller. In a small home with little linen storage, one spare set per bed is a realistic plan – and a full set takes noticeably less shelf space than a queen set. If the bed is pushed against a wall, fitted sheets with deep elastic corners make the wall side less of a struggle.

Frequently asked questions

How much bigger is a queen than a full?

A U.S. queen (60 × 80 in) is 7 in wider and 5 in longer than a full (53 × 75 in): about 5.7 sq ft more mattress, and about 8.6 sq ft more floor once 24 in access strips are included.

What is the smallest room for a queen bed?

With access on both sides and a walkable foot, the bed needs about 108 × 104 in. A 9 × 9 ft room is the practical minimum, but it leaves no space for other furniture.

Is a full bed big enough for two people?

It gives each sleeper 26.5 in of width. Some couples are fine with that; many prefer a queen at 30 in each.

Is a UK double the same as a US full?

Nearly: a UK double is 135 × 190 cm (about 53.1 × 74.8 in), a U.S. full is 53 × 75 in.
