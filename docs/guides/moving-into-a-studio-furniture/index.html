Moving into a studio: furniture to keep, sell, skip

Moving from a one-bedroom into a studio usually means losing a third of the furniture. Decide with measurements, not memories: draw the new room, add each piece with its use zone, and check the route in.

Last reviewed: October 20265 min readUnits: in + cm

The method

The question is not “will it fit?” but “will it fit with everything else, and can I still move around?”. Work through the pieces in order of necessity and stop adding when the walkway drops below 36 in – the clear width in ADA 2010 §403.5.1 that this site uses as its reference.

  1. Get the studio's plan with door positions and swings. Measure it yourself if possible – see how to measure a room.
  2. Measure every piece you are considering: outside dimensions with legs, handles and cushions.
  3. Place the bed first, with a 24 in strip on every side you use. It is the largest piece and has the fewest possible positions.
  4. Then the seating, then the table or desk with its chair zone, then storage.
  5. Check the route from the entry to bed, bathroom, kitchen and window after each piece.
  6. Check the delivery route for every large piece. A piece that does not get in is not a candidate.

Example: one-bedroom to 20 × 15 ft studio

From a one-bedroom with a queen bed, two nightstands, a sofa and armchair, a dining table for four, a desk, a dresser and a wardrobe, this studio kept the bed, one nightstand, the sofa, the desk and the dresser. The armchair, dining table and wardrobe did not fit without pushing a walkway below 36 in; the second nightstand would have blocked the bedside strip. A table for two would fit in place of the TV unit if eating at a table matters more than a TV.

Selling and replacing

  • Sell large and heavy pieces early; they take longest to sell and cost most to move.
  • Replace with dual-use pieces: sofa bed, extending table, ottoman with storage, bench instead of chairs.
  • Prefer legs to plinths – visible floor under furniture makes a room look larger, and robot vacuums can get under.
  • Prefer flat-pack for wardrobes – they pass any door and stair.

Getting it in

Studios are often in older buildings with narrow stairs and tight landings. The five delivery measurements – door clear width and height, diagonal depth, hallway widths at corners, headroom – tell you before moving day whether a sofa or wardrobe will make it. The delivery check tests all orientations automatically.

What happens to the things in the furniture?

Every dresser and wardrobe you leave behind holds things that need a new place. Before selling storage furniture, measure its volume (width × depth × height) and plan where that volume goes – the Storage Wall Audit shows how much storage a wall gives you at each reach height.

A two-week timeline

  • Two weeks before: measure the studio and the route; list every piece with dimensions; draw the plan.
  • Ten days before: decide keep/sell/skip; list the sell pieces; book the movers with the delivery measurements.
  • One week before: confirm that large pieces pass the route; disassemble what you can – legs, bed frames, wardrobe doors.
  • Moving day: place the bed first, as drawn; check the walkway with a tape before unpacking anything else.

Every piece that does not make it into the plan is a piece you do not have to carry up the stairs. And if the plan shows a gap where something is missing – a place to sit while putting on shoes, a surface beside the bed – buy that piece after you have lived in the studio for a few weeks, when you know how you actually use the room.

Frequently asked questions

How much furniture fits in a studio apartment?

Typically a bed, a sofa or loveseat, a small table or desk, one dresser or tall bookcase and a coffee table – as long as the route from the door to the bed stays about 36 in wide.

Should I bring my queen bed to a studio?

Usually yes for studios of about 250 sq ft or more, with one long side against a wall. Below that, consider a full or a wall bed.

What furniture should I sell before moving to a studio?

Large dining tables, king beds, oversized sofas and deep wardrobes are the usual candidates. Check each with the new floor plan first.

How do I know my furniture will get in?

Measure door clear widths, hallway widths at corners and headroom at stairs, and compare them with each piece, or use a delivery check tool.

Check your own room: draw the studio in the Clearance Planner and check each large piece with Will It Fit?.
