Small kitchen clearances: aisles, doors and dishwashers

A small kitchen works or fails on a handful of distances: the aisle between counters, the space beside the dishwasher door, the route past a seated person. The National Kitchen & Bath Association publishes all of them.

Last reviewed: October 20265 min readUnits: in + cm

The source

All figures on this page come from the NKBA Kitchen Planning Guidelines with Access Standards, published by the National Kitchen & Bath Association. They are recommendations for good design, not law; the document notes where state or local codes may apply. Guideline numbers are given so you can find each one in the PDF.

Work aisles and walkways

42 inWork aisle, one cook (1067 mm)NKBA Kitchen Planning Guideline 6 – 48 in (1219 mm) for multiple cooks

A work aisle is the space in front of counters and appliances where someone cooks. The NKBA recommends at least 42 in for one cook and 48 in for multiple cooks, measured between counter frontage, tall cabinets and appliances (guideline 6). A walkway is a route through the kitchen where no one works; it should be at least 36 in wide (guideline 7). Where two walkways meet at a right angle, the access standard recommends that one of them be 42 in.

The galley

A galley with two 25 in deep counter runs in an 8 ft (96 in) wide room leaves a 46 in aisle: above the 42 in one-cook figure, below the 48 in for two cooks. In practice that means one person cooks and a second can pass but not work back to back. A 7 ft 4 in room (88 in) gives exactly 38 in – a walkway, but tight for cooking. Below that, consider a single-wall kitchen with the other side free.

Dishwasher placement

Guideline 13 recommends placing the dishwasher within 36 in of the edge of the cleanup sink, and providing at least 21 in of standing space between the edge of the dishwasher and any counter, appliance or cabinet placed at a right angle to it. That second rule matters in L- and U-shaped kitchens: a dishwasher tucked right into the corner leaves you nowhere to stand while loading. The open door of a standard dishwasher projects roughly the depth of the unit into the aisle – about 2 ft – so the aisle in front of it is effectively closed while it is open.

Door and appliance interference

Guideline 2 says no entry door should interfere with the safe operation of appliances, and appliance doors should not interfere with one another. In a small kitchen, check three pairs: the oven door against the dishwasher door, the fridge door against the entry door, and any door against a person standing at the sink. Draw each appliance door as a zone and make sure no two overlap where both might be open at once.

Seating at a counter or table

For an eating counter or a small table in the kitchen, guideline 9 gives the knee space per person: 24 in wide in all cases, and 18 in deep at a 30 in high table, 15 in deep at a 36 in counter, 12 in deep at a 42 in bar. Behind the diners, plan 32 in to a wall if nobody passes, 36 in if people edge past and 44 in if they walk past (guideline 8). For a table in the living area, see the dining table guide.

Kitchens inside a studio

In a studio, the kitchen aisle is part of the room. Mark the 42 in in front of the counter as a zone where furniture may not stand – a table, sofa back or bed end inside it will get hit by an oven door. The walkway through the room can overlap with that zone; the planner treats it as walk-through.

Accessibility notes

The access standard part of the guidelines, based on ICC A117.1-2009, asks for a clear floor space of 30 × 48 in at each appliance, a 60 in clearance between opposing arms of a U-shaped kitchen and a 60 in turning space. If you are planning for a wheelchair user, those figures replace the ones above.

Quick check for your kitchen

  1. Measure between opposing counter fronts, handles included: 42 in or more?
  2. Open the dishwasher: 21 in from its edge to the nearest cabinet at a right angle?
  3. Open the oven, fridge and dishwasher one after another: does any door hit another or the entry door?
  4. If people eat in the kitchen: 32–44 in behind each seat, depending on traffic?

Frequently asked questions

How wide should a kitchen aisle be?

The NKBA recommends at least 42 in for one cook and 48 in for multiple cooks (Kitchen Planning Guideline 6). A walkway where nobody cooks should be at least 36 in.

How much space do you need beside a dishwasher?

At least 21 in of standing space between the edge of the dishwasher and any counter or appliance at a right angle to it (NKBA guideline 13).

How wide is a galley kitchen?

With two 25 in counters and a 42 in aisle, about 92 in (7 ft 8 in) wall to wall; with a 48 in aisle, about 98 in.

How much space behind a kitchen island stool?

NKBA guideline 8: 32 in to a wall with no traffic, 36 in to edge past, 44 in to walk past a seated person.

Check your own room: the galley kitchen opens in the Clearance Planner; adjust the run lengths to yours.
