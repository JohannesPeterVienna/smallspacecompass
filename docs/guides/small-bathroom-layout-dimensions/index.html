Bathrooms

Small bathroom layout dimensions

A small bathroom is a puzzle of a few fixed distances: from the toilet centerline to the wall, in front of each fixture, between two basins, and the inside of the shower. Here they are, with their sources and measured plans.

Last reviewed: October 20265 min readUnits: in + cm

Sources

The figures come from the NKBA Bathroom Planning Guidelines with Access Standards. The guidelines give a recommended value for good design and, where one exists, the code requirement that the NKBA cites from the 2015 International Residential Code (IRC) and International Plumbing Code; the access standards refer to ICC A117.1–2009. Your local code may be a later edition or differ – this page is for planning, not for approval.

Toilet

15 inToilet centerline to wall or fixture, code minimum (381 mm)IRC, cited in NKBA Bathroom Planning Guideline 20 – 18 in (457 mm) recommended

Measure from the centerline of the toilet to the nearest wall, fixture or obstacle on either side. The code minimum cited by the NKBA is 15 in; the recommendation is 18 in. A toilet between two walls therefore needs at least 30 in, better 36 in, of wall-to-wall width.

In front of fixtures

Space in front ofCode minimumNKBA recommended
Toilet, lavatory, bidet, tub21 in (533 mm)30 in (762 mm)
Shower entry24 in (610 mm)30 in (762 mm)

Guideline 4 recommends 30 in of clear floor space from the front edge of every fixture to any opposite fixture, wall or obstacle. The code minimums cited are 21 in in front of a lavatory, toilet, bidet and tub, and 24 in in front of a shower entry.

Basins

  • Single lavatory: centerline at least 20 in from a side wall or tall obstacle (recommended); 15 in code minimum (guideline 5).
  • Double lavatory: centerlines at least 36 in apart (recommended), 30 in code minimum (guideline 6).
  • For a 30 × 48 in clear floor space at the basin under the access standard, the centerline must be at least 24 in from the wall.

Two small layouts, checked against the minimums

Both plans are drawn from the same coordinates that produce the measurements below. The hatched strips are the code clear floor in front of each fixture and the side clearance to the code minimum from each centerline; nothing solid may stand in them. The site's test suite recalculates these strips for every plan before publishing.

The first plan is a 5 × 8 ft bathroom that meets the NKBA recommended values, not just the code minimums: the toilet centerline is 28 in from the nearest wall or fixture (recommended 18 in, code 15 in), there are 32 in of clear floor in front of the toilet and 39 in from the vanity front to the shower (recommended 30 in, code 21 in), and the vanity centerline is 20 in from the side wall (recommended 20 in). The shower entry is on the right-hand part of the shower, where the floor in front is clear. The door opens outward; a pocket door works too.

The second plan shows how tight the code minimums are. It works only with a small wall-hung basin (about 18 × 14 in) and with every key distance at the code value rather than the recommendation: toilet centerline 15 in from the side wall (recommended 18 in), basin centerline 15 in from the shower and from the wall (recommended 20 in), 21 in between the basin front and the toilet (recommended 30 in). The toilet has 32 in of clear floor in front of it, the shower entry 30 in (code 24 in). An inward-opening door would cross the shower entry; the plan assumes a sliding door. Treat this as the lower limit, not as a comfortable bathroom.

Doors

Bathroom doors are often the narrowest in the home. The ADA Standards define a 32 in clear width (§404.2.3) – useful as a reference for accessible bathrooms; in many homes interior bathroom doors are narrower. An outward-opening door frees the inside but must not swing into a corridor where someone walks. A pocket door frees both.

Quick check

  1. Toilet centerline to each side: 15 in minimum, 18 in better.
  2. Front of every fixture to the opposite wall or fixture: 21 in minimum (24 in at a shower), 30 in better.
  3. Shower inside: 30 × 30 in minimum, 36 × 36 in better.
  4. Door swing: does it hit a fixture or a person standing at the basin?

Bathtubs

A standard alcove bathtub is about 60 in long and 30–32 in wide, which is why so many small bathrooms are 5 ft wide: the tub fits wall to wall across one end. The space in front of the tub follows the same rule as the other fixtures – 21 in code minimum and 30 in recommended according to the guideline cited above. A tub with a shower over it needs the shower's own minimums only if it is used as a shower; a curtain or a fixed glass panel then defines the wet area.

Storage in a small bathroom

Floor space in front of fixtures cannot hold furniture, so storage moves up the walls. A mirror cabinet over the basin uses wall that is otherwise empty; shelves above the toilet should start high enough to clear the cistern and a standing person's head when bending. Apply the reach bands: daily toiletries between 15 and 48 in, spares above or below. Keep any shelf above the toilet shallow so that it does not intrude on the 18 in recommended clearance from the centerline at head height.

Measuring an existing bathroom

Measure from finished wall surface to finished wall surface – tiles count – and measure the toilet from the wall behind it to the center of the outlet, which decides which toilets fit as a replacement. Note the position of the waste pipes before planning any change: moving a toilet is the most expensive change in a bathroom, and many small-bathroom layouts are fixed by the soil stack rather than by space.

Frequently asked questions

How much space do you need beside a toilet?

At least 15 in from the centerline to any wall or fixture (IRC code minimum cited by the NKBA); 18 in is recommended.

How much space in front of a toilet?

21 in is the code minimum cited by the NKBA, 30 in is recommended.

What is the minimum shower size?

30 × 30 in (900 sq in) with room for a 30 in disc, under the IRC as cited in NKBA guideline 9. 36 × 36 in is recommended.

What is the smallest bathroom with a shower?

At the code minimums cited by the NKBA, about 5 × 5 ft: a 30 × 30 in shower, a toilet whose centerline is exactly 15 in from the wall and a small wall-hung basin (about 18 × 14 in) with 21 in in front of it, plus a sliding or outward-opening door. The NKBA recommended clearances do not fit in that room; 5 × 8 ft meets them.
