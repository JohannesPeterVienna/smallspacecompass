How to measure a room for furniture

Ten minutes with a tape measure saves a returned sofa. This is the method used for every plan on this site, with a printable checklist and a sketch template.

Last reviewed: October 20265 min readUnits: in + cm

What you need

  • A steel tape measure at least 25 ft (8 m) long; a laser measure is faster for long walls but cannot measure small offsets.
  • This checklist, printed, or squared paper.
  • A second person helps on walls longer than about 10 ft.

1. Sketch first, measure second

Draw the room outline before measuring – roughly, not to scale. Mark every door, window, radiator, built-in cupboard and column. Number the walls clockwise from the entry door. Writing measurements on a sketch is faster and less error-prone than a list, and it shows immediately when something is missing.

2. Measure every wall, at furniture height

Measure each wall from inside corner to inside corner, about 3 ft above the floor – that is where furniture meets the wall, above the skirting. Measure opposite walls separately: in older buildings they are often different by an inch or more. Write the smaller figure on the plan if furniture has to fit along the whole length.

3. Measure both diagonals

Corner to corner, both ways. If the two diagonals are equal, the room is rectangular. If they differ by more than about an inch, the room is out of square: a wardrobe that fits snugly at one end of a wall may not fit at the other. Note it on the plan.

4. Doors: position, clear width and swing

  • Offset: from the nearest corner to the edge of the door frame.
  • Clear width: with the door open 90°, from the face of the door to the opposite stop – the method in ADA 2010 §404.2.3. This is the figure that matters for deliveries.
  • Hinge side and swing direction: which side the hinges are on and whether the door opens into the room. The swing claims a quarter circle with a radius equal to the leaf width.
  • Clear height: floor or threshold to the underside of the head stop.

5. Windows

Offset from the nearest corner, width of the opening, sill height above the floor, and how the sash opens. A window that opens inwards needs a clear zone in front of it; a sill lower than about 30 in limits what can stand beneath it. Radiators are often under windows – measure their depth and height too.

6. Everything that is fixed

Outlets, switches, thermostats, vents, columns, pipes, built-in cupboards, the depth of skirting boards and window boards. For each, note the distance from the nearest corner and its height. Outlets matter more than people expect: a bed or wardrobe in front of the only outlet on a wall is a daily annoyance.

7. Heights

Ceiling height in at least two places, beams and soffits, and the height of anything that limits tall furniture – a sloped ceiling, a window head, a light fitting. For storage walls, the reach range of 15–48 in from ADA 2010 §308 is a useful reference for what you can reach without bending or a stool; see Vertical storage by reach.

8. The delivery route

Every door, hallway and stair between the street and the room: clear widths, hallway widths at each corner, headroom at landings. These decide what can come in at all. The sofa delivery guide explains the five measurements in detail.

Common mistakes

  • Measuring at the floor, where skirting boards make the room look 1–2 in narrower – or at the ceiling, where crown moulding does.
  • Measuring the door leaf instead of the clear opening.
  • Forgetting the door swing – the most common reason a planned layout fails.
  • Rounding up. Round down to the nearest 1/4 in for space, up for furniture.
  • Measuring furniture without handles, feet and cushions.

Turn the sketch into a plan

A sketch with numbers is enough to shop with, but a scaled plan shows problems that numbers hide. Enter the walls, doors and windows in the Clearance Planner and add the pieces you own or want to buy, each with its use zone. The planner draws the door swings and measures the narrowest point of every route, so you see at once whether a sofa that “fits” on paper leaves a walkable path. Export the drawing as SVG to print it, or as JSON to keep a copy that you can reopen later and adjust when you buy something new.

Keep the original measurements with the plan. When a delivery driver or a landlord asks for a dimension, the sketch with its handwritten numbers is the record that counts.

Frequently asked questions

Where should I measure a wall?

At about 3 ft above the floor, corner to corner between the inside faces – above skirting boards and below any crown moulding.

Why measure the diagonals?

To check whether the room is square. Unequal diagonals mean the walls are not at right angles, which matters for tight-fitting furniture.

How do I measure a door for furniture delivery?

Open it to 90° and measure from the face of the door to the opposite stop. That clear width is smaller than the door leaf.

Should I use a laser measure?

For long walls and ceiling heights it is faster and accurate. For offsets, door clear widths and furniture, a steel tape is easier.
