How much walkway space do you need around furniture?

Plan main routes at 36 in (915 mm) clear, allow short pinches down to 32 in, and add the space that chairs, drawers and doors need before you measure what is left. Here is where those numbers come from and how to apply them in a small room.

Last reviewed: October 20265 min readUnits: in + cm

The short answer

36 inMain walkway, clear width (915 mm)ADA 2010 §403.5.1 – walking surfaces; also NKBA kitchen guideline 7

A walkway that people use every day – from the front door to the kitchen, from the bedroom door to the bed – should be at least 36 in (915 mm) wide between the nearest surfaces. That figure is the minimum clear width of walking surfaces in section 403.5.1 of the 2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design. Private homes are usually not required to follow those standards, but 36 in is the most widely used published number for a comfortable one-person route, and the NKBA Kitchen Planning Guidelines use the same figure for kitchen walkways (guideline 7).

The same ADA section allows the route to narrow to 32 in (815 mm) for a length of no more than 24 in, provided those pinch points are separated by at least 48 in of full 36 in width. In a home that translates into a useful rule: squeezing past the corner of a nightstand or a door frame is fine; a 32 in corridor along the full length of a sofa is not.

Measure the gap that is actually left

Most walkway problems in small rooms come from measuring the wrong thing. The gap between a sofa and a coffee table is not the gap between their feet; it is the distance between the front of the seat cushion and the table edge – minus the space someone's legs take while seated. Before you measure a walkway, subtract every use zone that sits next to it:

Corners and turns

A route that turns around the end of a bed or a kitchen island needs more width than a straight one. ADA §403.5.2 asks for 42 in approaching and leaving a 180° turn around an element narrower than 48 in, and 48 in at the turn itself. The NKBA access standard recommends that where two kitchen walkways meet at a right angle, one of them should be 42 in wide. You do not need to apply these figures literally in a studio, but they explain a common experience: the corner of a bed on the way to the window always feels tighter than the same width along a straight wall.

When two people need to pass

The 36 in figure assumes one person walking. Where people regularly pass each other or work back to back, plan more. The NKBA guidelines are the best published reference for homes: 42 in for a one-cook kitchen work aisle, 48 in for multiple cooks (guideline 6), and 44 in to walk past someone seated at a table (guideline 8b). Behind a dining chair where people only edge past sideways, NKBA allows 36 in.

How to measure an existing walkway

  1. Walk the route you use most – door to bed, door to desk, bed to bathroom – and note where you turn your shoulders or slow down. Those are your pinch points.
  2. At each one, measure between the two nearest surfaces at hip height, not at the floor: sofa arms, table tops and open drawers stick out further than legs and plinths.
  3. Open every door and drawer that faces the route and measure again.
  4. Compare with the table above. If a pinch is shorter than about two feet and only one, it is usually acceptable.

Fixes that gain the most width

  • Turn the chair, not the desk. Rotating a desk so that the chair zone points into a corner or along a wall frees the full 32 in that a chair needs.
  • Change the door swing. Re-hanging a door to swing against the nearest wall, or replacing it with a pocket or barn door, can free a quarter circle of up to 36 in radius.
  • Shallower pieces along routes. A 12 in deep bookcase instead of a 16 in dresser along a corridor gains 4 in where it matters most.
  • Round tables at pinch points. A round table loses its corners exactly where people walk past.
  • Lift storage off the floor. Wall-mounted shelves above 34 in or so keep the floor clear at hip height – check the reach guide for the heights you can still use.

In very small studios it is sometimes impossible to keep every route at 36 in. Then prioritise: the route from the entry to the bed and to the bathroom should be the widest, because you use it at night and in a hurry. Routes to a window or a rarely used cupboard can be tighter.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum walkway width in a home?

There is no single legal minimum for walkways between furniture in private homes. The most widely used published figure is 36 in (915 mm), the clear width of walking surfaces in the 2010 ADA Standards (§403.5.1). Short pinches down to 32 in for up to 24 in of length are allowed there.

How much space should be between a sofa and a coffee table?

Enough for legs and for walking past: this site uses an 18 in legroom zone in front of the seat as a planning assumption. If the gap is also a walkway, it needs the legroom plus the walkway width.

How much space do you need behind a dining chair?

The NKBA Kitchen Planning Guidelines recommend 32 in from table edge to wall where no one passes, 36 in to edge past a seated person and 44 in to walk past (guideline 8).

Is 30 inches enough for a walkway?

For a short squeeze past a corner, it is tight but usable. For a main route it is below both the 36 in ADA width and the 32 in pinch allowance, so most people will turn sideways.
